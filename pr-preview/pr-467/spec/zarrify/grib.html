GRIB¶

The Zarr layout of a GRIB edition 2 file: the gridded fields of a weather or climate model’s output, as labeled arrays with CF coordinates and GeoZarr georeferencing. What all of refkv’s conventions share is in spec/conventions.md, cited here as “conventions §n”. How refkv produces this layout as a reference store, which files it reads and which inputs it rejects, is the GRIB profile, Part 2 below. The format is WMO’s FM 92 GRIB edition 2, in WMO-No. 306, Manual on Codes, Volume I.2, and its code and template tables (wmo-im/GRIB2), cited here as “FM 92”; a template is named by its number, as 3.30 (grid definition template 30), and an octet by its number in its section, from 1.

Convention version: 0 (until release, conventions §1) · UUID: c9bbe039-8dfa-49f9-a655-756f822a42d6 · Schema: schema.json

Changes: changes/grib/, one file per change.

Added 2026-10-30.

This document has two parts (conventions §11.1).

Like MP3, HDF5 and NumPy, GRIB’s fields are not images: the hierarchy holds no OME-NGFF metadata. As with SAFE, it is georeferenced with the GeoZarr conventions. The values stay in the file: each chunk is one field’s message, decoded by the codec refkv.grib (§3.4).

2. The source¶

2.1 Messages and fields¶

A GRIB file is a sequence of messages. A message is section 0 (16 octets: GRIB, two reserved octets, the discipline, the edition, 2, and the message’s length, octets 9–16), the sections 1 to 7, and section 8 (7777). Each section starts with its length (4 octets) and its number (1 octet). A message describes one or more fields: after a field’s section 7, the message may restate sections 2 to 7, 3 to 7 or 4 to 7 for the next (FM 92 regulation 92.1.3). A field’s sections are the last of each number at or before its section 7.

section

what this convention reads of it

1, identification

the reference time (octets 13–19); the rest identifies the field

2, local use

nothing (it is kept in the mirror)

3, grid definition

the grid (§2.4)

4, product definition

the parameter, the surfaces, the step, the ensemble member and the statistic (§2.2, §2.3)

5, data representation

the number of values and the packing template

6, bitmap

the bitmap indicator (octet 6)

7, data

nothing (the codec decodes it)

2.2 Identity¶

A field’s identity is the octets that say what it is, all but those that say where it lies along the dimensions of §2.3: the discipline; section 1 from octet 6, but the reference time’s octets; and section 4 from octet 6, with the octets of the unit and forecast time (18–22), the surfaces’ scale factors and values (24–28, 30–34), an ensemble member’s number, and a statistic’s end time and its count of missing values set to 0. A variable’s fields have one identity and one grid, and each of the two surfaces either has a value in every field or in none (§3.1).

2.3 Dimensions¶

A field’s coordinates are:

  • time: the reference time, in seconds since 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z;

  • step: for a template without a statistic, the forecast time in seconds (its unit, code table 4.4: a minute, an hour, a day, 3, 6 or 12 hours, or a second); for one with a statistic, the statistic’s end less the reference time, in seconds;

  • level: the first surface’s value, value × 10^−factor (the factor a signed octet, its high bit the sign, the value 4 unsigned octets; in binary64, value / 10^factor for a factor of at least 0 and value × 10^−factor otherwise, the power of 10 a product of tens, each multiplication rounded; none when the type is 255, the factor 0xff or the value all ones), and the second’s, as the level’s bound;

  • number: the ensemble member’s number, for 4.1 and 4.11.

A field whose statistic has more than one time range (its count, not 1) is not presented (statistic); a statistic’s length is part of the variable’s name (§3.2).

2.4 Grids¶

A field is presented only when its section 3 defines its grid by a template (octet 6, 0) of one of these, whose Ni × Nj is its number of points (octets 7–10), with no list of points per row (octet 11, 0) unless it is a reduced grid below; otherwise it is not presented (grid_source, reduced, grid, grid_size, §2.6). Two grids are one when their sections 3 are equal from octet 6 but the scanning mode’s bits 3 and 4 (0x20, 0x10), which change the order of the points in the file, not in the decoded field:

Angles are in 10^−6 degree, or for 3.0, 3.1 and 3.40 in units of the basic angle and its subdivisions (octets 39–46) when both are given. The scanning mode’s bit 1 (0x80) runs i from east to west, and bit 2 (0x40) runs j from south to north; an increment takes the sign of its direction. A 3.1, 3.10, 3.20 or 3.30 field whose Earth (code table 3.2, octet 15) is not of a shape of the table below, or a shape whose octets 16–30 it needs are missing (a scale factor 0xff or a value of all ones), or a 3.20 or 3.30 field whose projection is bipolar (octet 64, bit 2), is not presented (earth). A scaled value of octets 16–30 is its 4 octets, unsigned, scaled by the signed octet before them as a level’s value is (§2.3).

A 3.20 field whose LaD is not in its pole’s hemisphere (above 0 and at most 90 for the north pole, below 0 and at least −90 for the south), a 3.10 field whose LaD is not between −90 and 90, or either with an increment that is 0 or missing, is not presented (projection). Octets 61–64 of 3.10, the orientation of the grid, are not read: NOAA’s NBM writes a central meridian there, on a grid whose i runs along the parallels, and a Mercator grid with another orientation would be read as if it did too.

Gaussian rows. A 3.40 grid’s rows are of the 2N latitudes of §4, numbered from 0 north to south, and Nj of them may be presented: its first row is the one nearest La1, and the rows that follow run south, or north with the scanning mode’s bit 2. Such a field whose N is 0 or more than 8192, a bound on the time its rows take to compute, whose La1 is not within 9 / N degrees (a tenth of the rows’ spacing) of a row, or whose rows run past a pole, is not presented (gaussian). Its longitude increment comes from its corners, not from Di, which producers round (CFSv2 writes 0.938 for 0.9375, which puts its last point 0.19° off): 360 / Ni when (Lo2 − Lo1) + 360 / Ni is within a tenth of 360 / Ni of 360, else (Lo2 − Lo1) / (Ni − 1), with 360 added or subtracted so that the span runs i’s way, and 0 when Ni is 1.

Reduced grids. A 3.0 or 3.40 grid with a list of the points along each parallel (octet 11, the width in octets of each number, at most 8; octet 12, 1) is presented as a sequence of points, when: Ni is missing (all ones); the list, from octet 73, holds a number for each of its Nj rows, which add up to its number of points; its scanning mode has none of bits 1, 3 and 4 set; and its rows go round the Earth: (Lo2 − Lo1) + 360 / m, with 360 added when Lo2 − Lo1 is negative and m its longest row, is within a tenth of 360 / m of 360. The points of a row of c are at Lo1 + i × 360 / c, i from 0, and the rows’ latitudes are a 3.40’s above (N and La1, within its rules) or a 3.0’s (La1 and Dj, or from La2). Any other grid with a list is not presented (reduced), including a sub-area of a reduced grid, whose rows do not go round the Earth.

2.5 Values¶

A field’s values are those refkv.grib decodes from its message (§3.4): binary64, NaN where its bitmap or its packing says a value is missing, in the grid’s row order. A field whose packing (section 5’s template) is not one the codec decodes (5.0, 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 5.40, 5.41 or 5.42) is not presented (packing). The profile reads no more of section 7 than its header, so a 5.40 field whose codestream the codec does not decode (the 9/7 transform, more than 20 bits) is presented, and reading its chunk is a decode error (§3.4).

3. The hierarchy¶

The root has one group per grid, grid<g>, of the presented fields of the grids, numbered from 0 in the order of the grids’ first fields (two grids are one as §2.4 says). A grid’s group holds its variables and its coordinates. xarray opens a grid’s group as a dataset (xr.open_zarr(store, group="grid0")), and the hierarchy as a DataTree.

3.1 Variables¶

A grid’s presented fields are its variables’, by identity (§2.2), each variable in the order of its first field. A variable is a float64 array of NaN fill whose dimensions are, in order:

  • time and step, always;

  • its level dimension (§3.2), when its fields’ first surfaces have values;

  • number, when its template has an ensemble member;

  • the grid’s: y and x for 3.10, 3.20 and 3.30; latitude and longitude for 3.0 and 3.40; rlat and rlon for 3.1; and point alone for a reduced grid and 3.101, whose length is the grid’s number of points.

Its shape is its group’s coordinates’ lengths along its dimensions, its chunk shape 1 along each but the grid’s, which it takes whole; its codecs are [{"name": "refkv.grib"}]. A chunk is a field at its coordinates; a cell no field has has no chunk, and reads as NaN.

Repeated fields. A field whose cell another field of its identity already holds (a file may hold two fields with the same sections 1 to 5, as NCEP’s GFS does) goes to the next copy: the first field of each cell is in the variable, the second in a second variable named as the first with _2, and so on. No field is dropped.

A variable’s attributes are long_name and units, when §3.2’s table names its parameter; coordinates, "valid_time", or on a reduced grid "valid_time latitude longitude"; and GRIB_centre, GRIB_discipline, GRIB_parameterCategory, GRIB_parameterNumber, GRIB_productDefinitionTemplateNumber, GRIB_typeOfFirstFixedSurface and GRIB_typeOfSecondFixedSurface, the numbers that identify it, and for a statistic GRIB_typeOfStatisticalProcessing and GRIB_lengthOfTimeRange (its length in seconds, or null), under cfgrib’s names.

GribVariable: an ArrayDocument with the data type float64, the fill value "NaN", the codecs above, the dimension_names of its dimensions and the attributes above.

3.2 Names¶

A variable’s name is its parameter’s short name, then _ and its level type’s name (unless its first surface’s type is 255), then _ and its qualifier, if any, then, for a statistic, _, the process’s name (code table 4.10, by the table grib/statistics.tsv: avg, accum, max, min, diff, rms, sd, cov, diffStartEnd, ratio, anomaly, sum, else stat<n>) and its length (<h>h, <m>min or <s>s, the first that divides it), as t_isobaric, tp_surface_accum6h. A name an earlier variable or coordinate of the group has takes _2, _3, … .

  • Short names are those of the table grib/parameters.tsv, part of this convention, by the centre, the discipline, the category and the number (its columns centre, discipline, category, number, short_name, name and units, tab-separated, one row per parameter): the centre’s own row (NCEP’s, 7, and ECMWF’s, 98, where ecCodes names a parameter otherwise than WMO), else WMO’s, the row of centre 0. A parameter it does not name is d<discipline>_c<category>_p<number>. Its name and units are the variable’s long_name and units (§3.1). The table was generated by spec/tools/grib_names.py from ecCodes 2.50.0’s definitions (Apache 2.0); regenerating it from another release is a change of this convention.

  • Qualifiers follow the level type for a product that is a probability (4.5, 4.9: prob and, by code table 4.9, lt<lower>, gt<upper>, <lower>to<upper>, gt<lower> or lt<upper>, a limit’s shortest decimal with p for its point and m for a minus), a percentile (4.6, 4.10: p<percentile>) or a derived forecast (4.2, 4.12, code table 4.7: ensmean, enswmean, enssd, enssdn, ensspread, enslai, enscmean, ensiqr, ensmin, ensmax, else ens<code>).

  • Level types are named by the table grib/surfaces.tsv, part of this convention, of code table 4.5 (its columns centre, type, name and units, the units empty for a type whose values have none): surface, isobaric (in Pa), heightAboveGround, depthBelowLand, and so on, the rows of centre 0 for every centre, and NCEP’s local types, the rows of centre 7, for its centre alone; another is level<type>. A level dimension’s units (§3.3) are its type’s. A layer of two surfaces of one type is the type’s name with Layer; of two types, the first’s name, To, and the second’s, capitalized.

GribCoordinate: an ArrayDocument of the table’s row, a float64 coordinate with the fill value "NaN".

3.4 The codec refkv.grib¶

A GRIB edition 2 message holds one or more fields, each a grid of values packed by one of FM 92’s data representation templates, with the grid, the packing’s parameters and the bitmap of missing points in the message’s own sections. The array-to-bytes codec refkv.grib decodes a field of a message.

The codec. {"name": "refkv.grib", "configuration": C}, where C, which may be absent, is a GribConfiguration:

member

type

presence

value

field

integer

optional

the field to decode, from 0, in the order of the message’s sections 7; 0 when absent

There are no other members. A chunk’s number of values is section 3’s number of points, at most 2^31. On a grid of rows, its shape is 1 along every dimension but its last two, which are the grid’s Nj and Ni (section 3’s number of points along a meridian and along a parallel, octets 35–38 and 31–34 of templates 3.0, 3.1, 3.10, 3.20, 3.30 and 3.40), whose product is that number. On a grid with no such rows, a reduced grid (3.0 or 3.40 with a list of the points along each parallel, section 3’s octet 11 not 0 and octet 12, 1) or 3.101, its shape is 1 along every dimension but its last, which is that number. The array’s data type is float64 or float32.

A chunk’s bytes are one message, sections 0 to 8, as a GRIB file holds it, and no more: the chunk’s length is the message’s.

Decoding a chunk.

  1. The field. The sections are walked from section 0; the field’s sections 3, 5, 6 and 7 are the last of each number at or before its section 7. A message that does not parse (a section past the message’s length, no 7777 at its end) or has too few fields is an error.

  2. Unpacking. Section 5’s template gives the field’s n values (octets 6–9):

    • 5.0, simple packing, 5.2, complex packing, and 5.3, complex packing with spatial differencing of order 1 or 2: scaled integers X, each value (R + X × 2^E) × 10^−D, where R is octets 12–15, an IEEE binary32, and E and D octets 16–17 and 18–19, signed integers (sign and magnitude), computed in binary64: 2^E exactly, and 10^−D as D successive divisions of 1 by 10 (for a negative D, −D successive multiplications), which for D ≥ 6 is not always the binary64 nearest 10^−D, but is what ecCodes computes. A bit width of 0 (octet 20), or complex packing with no groups, makes every value R, as ecCodes, NCEP’s g2c and their encoders read it, though FM 92’s formula would scale it. Complex packing’s missing values (missing value management, octet 23, 1 or 2) are NaN; with spatial differencing, the differences run over the values that are not missing, and the first values (octets 1–6 of section 7’s start) are unsigned, the overall minimum signed.

    • 5.4: IEEE floating point values, binary32 or binary64 (octet 12), as they are.

    • 5.40: section 7 is a JPEG 2000 codestream (ITU-T T.800 | ISO/IEC 15444-1, not a JP2 file) of X: one unsigned component, not subsampled, of at most 20 bits, whose image has n samples (Ni × Nj, or n × 1 with a bitmap, as NCEP’s g2c writes it), of the reversible 5/3 wavelet transform, lossless or truncated at a rate (ecCodes’ lossy 5.40, octet 22 = 1, keeps the 5/3 transform). X is the samples OpenJPEG decodes, which ecCodes calls: the 5/3 transform in integers, a truncated coefficient at the midpoint of its interval, the samples clamped to the precision’s range. Then as 5.0. The irreversible 9/7 transform is an error (decoders compute it in floating point, each in its own order, and their samples differ by 1 at some points), as are samples of more than 20 bits (within which a decoder that computes the 5/3 transform in binary32 is exact).

    • 5.41: section 7 is a PNG image of X, not interlaced and with no palette, a gray sample of 1 to 16 bits, or an RGB or RGBA sample of 24 or 32 bits as one integer; then as 5.0.

    • 5.42: section 7 is a CCSDS 121.0-B adaptive entropy coded stream of X, unsigned, of the flags, block size and reference sample interval of octets 22–25; then as 5.0.

    Any other template (the spectral 5.50 and 5.51; 5.61; 5.200), or 5.4 of another precision, is an error.

  3. Placing. With a bitmap (section 6’s indicator 0; or 254, the bitmap of the last earlier field of the message whose indicator is 0), the n values fill, in order, the points whose bit is 1, and the others are NaN; the number of bits that are 1 must be n. Without one (255), n must be the grid’s number of points. An indicator from 1 to 253 is an error.

  4. Ordering. The points, in the file’s order, are its lines: Nj lines of Ni points, or, when bit 3 (0x20) of section 3’s scanning mode is set, Ni lines of Nj points (the points consecutive along j). When bit 4 (0x10) is set, so that every other line runs the opposite way, those lines (the second, the fourth, …) are reversed. Then, with bit 3, the lines are transposed, so that the result is Nj rows of Ni points. Bits 1 and 2, the directions of i and j, are kept: the first row is the one the file gives first. Scanning-mode bits 5 to 8 (0x0F) must be 0. A reduced grid’s scanning mode must have none of bits 3 to 8 set, and it and 3.101, which has none, are one line of their points, in the file’s order.

  5. Type. The values are converted to the array’s data type: float64 as they are, float32 to nearest.

The codec has no encoder.

4. Georeferencing¶

A grid’s group carries the GeoZarr proj and spatial conventions’ members, as SAFE’s resolution groups do (spec/zarrify/safe.md §5), but a reduced grid’s, which has proj alone, and a 3.101 grid’s, which has neither.

The Lambert projection. (x0, y0) is the Lambert conformal conic projection of the first point, (La1, Lo1), with its origin at (Latin1, LoV) (Snyder, Map Projections: A Working Manual, 1987, equations 15-1 to 15-10 and 14-15). It is computed in binary64 (spec/zarrify.md §1.3), each operation in the order written, with musl’s sin, cos, tan, log, pow and sqrt (as the Rust crate libm 0.2 implements them), so that every implementation computes the same bits. π is the binary64 value nearest π, r(d) = d × (π / 180) (the quotient rounded first), and the angles are in degrees (§2.4):

  • the Earth (§2.4): for a sphere, a its radius and e = 0; for an ellipsoid, a its semi-major axis, f = 1 / inverse flattening and e = sqrt(f × (2 − f));

  • φ = r(La1), φ1 = r(Latin1), φ2 = r(Latin2);

  • m(x) = cos(x) / sqrt(1 − e × e × sin(x) × sin(x));

  • t(x) = tan(π/4 − x / 2) / pow((1 − e × sin(x)) / (1 + e × sin(x)), e / 2), π/4 the binary64 value nearest π/4;

  • n = sin(φ1) when φ1 = φ2, else n = (log(m(φ1)) − log(m(φ2))) / (log(t(φ1)) − log(t(φ2)));

  • F = m(φ1) / (n × pow(t(φ1), n));

  • ρ = a × F × pow(t(φ), n) and ρ0 = a × F × pow(t(φ1), n);

  • θ = n × r(L), where L = ((Lo1 − LoV) + 180) mod 360 − 180, and x mod 360 is the exact remainder of x / 360 with the sign of x (C’s fmod), plus 360 when it is negative (Rust’s f64::rem_euclid);

  • x0 = ρ × sin(θ) and y0 = ρ0 − ρ × cos(θ).

The polar stereographic projection. (x0, y0) is the polar stereographic projection of (La1, Lo1), true to scale at LaD (Snyder, equations 21-33 to 21-36, 14-15 and 15-9), computed the same way with a, e, m and t above, and s = −1 when the projection’s pole is the south pole, else 1:

  • ρ = 2 × a × t(r(s × La1)) / sqrt(pow(1 + e, 1 + e) × pow(1 − e, 1 − e)) when LaD is 90 or −90;

  • else ρ = a × m(c) × t(r(s × La1)) / t(c), where c = r(s × LaD);

  • θ = r(L), where L is Lo1 − LoV in [−180, 180) as above;

  • x0 = s × ρ × sin(θ) and y0 = −ρ × cos(θ): whichever the pole, y runs towards it along LoV.

That is how NCEP’s south-polar grids place their points: the first point of its sea ice analysis (seaice.t00z.south12psg.grib2), 36.866°S 139.806°E with LoV 100°, scanning j northward, puts the pole inside the grid and its ice in the Weddell, Ross and Amundsen Seas only so. ecCodes 2.50 runs x and y the other way, off Antarctica, and FM 92’s note that latitude increases with y along LoV holds only of a north pole.

The Mercator projection. (x0, y0) is the Mercator projection of (La1, Lo1), true to scale at LaD, its central meridian Greenwich (Snyder, equations 7-6, 7-7, 14-15 and 15-9), computed the same way with a, e, m and t above: k = a × m(r(LaD)), x0 = k × r(L), where L is Lo1 in [−180, 180) as above, and y0 = −k × log(t(r(La1))). Its grid may run past 180°: its x then exceeds the projection’s range, as Lo1 + i × dx does.

The Gaussian latitudes. The 2N latitudes of a 3.40 grid are, from north to south, the zeros of the Legendre polynomial P of degree 2N, computed in binary64 with musl’s cos and asin, each operation in the order written, by Newton’s method, 5 times from an asymptotic estimate. For row k, from 0, the latitude is −(the latitude of row 2N − 1 − k) when k is at least N, and otherwise:

  • x = cos(π × (k + 0.75) / (2N + 0.5)), then 5 times:

    • p0 = 1, p = x, then for j from 2 to 2N, q = ((2 × j − 1) × x × p − (j − 1) × p0) / j, p0 = p, p = q;

    • d = 2N × (p0 − x × p) / (1 − x × x) and x = x − p / d;

  • the latitude is asin(x) × (180 / π) degrees, 180 / π the quotient rounded first.

The row nearest La1 (§2.4) is k, the integer nearest (90 − La1) × (2N + 0.5) / 180 − 0.75, halves away from zero, which must lie from −0.5 to below 2N.

The grid’s evenly spaced positions are its y and x coordinates: y0 + j × dy and x0 + i × dx. The values are in the file’s order (§2.5): a grid that scans north to south has a descending latitude.

Unstructured grids. A 3.101 field’s points are placed by a separate grid file, which the group’s GRIB_uuidOfHGrid names and the file does not hold. The values are presented by their index in that file.

7. What the layout does not express¶

  • GRIB edition 1, and the fields of §2.6.

  • 2-D latitudes and longitudes of a projected or rotated grid: a reader computes them from the CRS.

  • A 3.101 grid’s positions, which are in another file (§4).

  • A field’s position in a file of many fields as a dimension of its own: the many files of a forecast run, stacked along time and step, are a later profile’s.

  • Which of complex packing’s two missing values a missing value was: both are NaN.

Part 2. The profile¶

The GRIB profile reads a GRIB edition 2 file, and the index beside it when there is one, and writes the layout of Part 1.

8. GRIB profile¶

A GRIB file is a file read as a store (spec/zarrify.md §1.8): the store URL is the GRIB file’s directory, its objects are the GRIB file, the input, at its key, and the index files the run probes for beside it (§8.2), and the root’s source.url is the GRIB file’s object URL.

8.1 Choosing the profile¶

A file whose first 4 bytes are GRIB and whose eighth is 2 is GRIB’s (the registry’s sniff order, spec/zarrify.md §1.2).

8.2 Finding the messages¶

The run probes for two index files beside the GRIB file, in this order: <key>.idx (NOAA’s: lines n:offset:…, or n.k:offset:… for each field of a message of several) and, when the key ends in .grib2, .grb2, .grib or .grb, the key with that suffix replaced by .index (ECMWF’s: JSON lines with _offset). It reads every index found that is not empty and at most 16 MiB (2^24 bytes) long, whole, and the mirror holds each one read (§5.2). The run uses the first index, in that order, that lists offsets, the first 0 and all below the file’s size. With one, the run reads the first 16 KiB of each listed message, from 4 bytes before it, at once; with none, or from the first offset where the file and the index disagree (a listed offset at which no message starts, or none the checks of §8.4 accept, or that lies inside a message), it walks the file: 64 KiB from where the last message ended (more, where a section is longer), a message’s length taking it to the next. Every message is read and checked (§8.4) whichever way it was found, so an index changes the reads and not the output, but for §5.1’s index and the mirror’s held files. The index files are optional siblings (spec/zarrify.md §1.9): one outside the input’s scope is absent.

Bytes where a message is due that do not start with GRIB are padding: the bytes to the next GRIB, or to the file’s end, which MUST be at most 4096 bytes (§8.4).

8.3 Reading each message¶

Of each message, the run reads sections 0 to 5 whole, the first 6 octets of each section 6, and the first 5 of each section 7, reading again where a head did not hold them.

8.5 Chunks¶

GribChunk: a field’s chunk is a message that holds that field alone:

  • the field’s message, one range of the GRIB file, when it holds no other field;

  • else a message made of the field’s sections: a literal section 0 (GRIB, two zero octets, the discipline, 2, and the new length), the ranges of its sections 1 to 7 (a reused bitmap’s section 6 in place of its own), and a literal 7777.

The output’s one source is the GRIB file’s object URL, with a size pin (spec/zarrify.md §1.8); an index is no source.

8.6 Summary¶

The run’s summary: messages, fields, grids, arrays (each variable’s path, shape and count of fields), chunks, unpresented (the count of each reason), index, and the store run’s counts.

8.7 The description¶

This section is not part of the convention: nothing it specifies is written in an output. Beside the IR, the store run’s parser returns a description of the file: what it found of its messages and fields, in the terms of §2, for a projection to read with the IR and its stored arrays (spec/stored-array.md §7). Its JSON Schema is grib/description.json. It holds none of an output’s decisions: which fields are presented, for the convention’s reasons packing, bitmap and values (§2.6), how they make variables, their names and coordinates (§3), and the root’s source metadata (§5.1) are the projection’s. Those decisions are refkv’s plan of the file, made from the description alone by the function the convention’s projection calls; so a file of which no field is presented (§8.4) is rejected where the projection makes its plan. A layout that wants another hierarchy makes its own from the description.
