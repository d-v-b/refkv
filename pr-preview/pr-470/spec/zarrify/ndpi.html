NDPI¶

The Zarr layout of a Hamamatsu NDPI slide, and its source model: the elements of its IR, which refkv_source holds as the IR mirror. NDPI is a variant of TIFF; this convention builds on the TIFF convention. What all of refkv’s conventions share is in spec/conventions.md, cited here as “conventions §n”. How refkv produces this layout as a reference store is the NDPI profile, Part 2 below.

Convention version: 0 (until release, conventions §1) · UUID: 6cac71ef-dbb2-4acd-b60c-00389aa4238a · Schema: schema.json

Changes: changes/ndpi/, one file per change.

Changes before 2026-10-22 are recorded in the revision log of VIRTUALIZE.md, this document’s earlier name, up to revision 29.

This document has two parts (conventions §11.1).

Part 1. The convention¶

This convention gives a layout only to the files that meet its requirements. Where it says that “the input is rejected”, or that something MUST hold, a file that fails has no layout under this convention, and the profile rejects it.

2. The source¶

An NDPI file is a little-endian classic TIFF (magic 42) whose IFDs have 64-bit offsets (§7 says how they are read). Its IFDs are those of its main chain, in order. Of duplicate tags in an IFD, the first is used.

The layout uses these tags, besides ImageWidth (256), ImageLength (257), BitsPerSample (258), Compression (259), PhotometricInterpretation (262) and SamplesPerPixel (277), whose defaults are the TIFF convention’s; field types and counts are as the profile checks them:

(Tag 65433 is in fluorescence and brightfield files alike, with no known meaning. It is kept in the source model, like every other tag.)

An image’s focal offset is tag 65424, ZOffsetFromSlideCenter, in nanometers, signed: its first value, when its field type is SHORT, LONG, SSHORT or SLONG (3, 4, 8, 9), it lies within the file, and it is at most 2^53 − 1 from 0. Otherwise the image has none. Like the acquisition tags it never rejects the input, and it is read only when two images have one Magnification (§3). Hamamatsu’s description of the format lists it as Physical Z, an SLONG, the focus position in nanometers, and says that each focal plane has its own pyramid levels. The name is tifffile’s, which reads it as nanometers too; OpenSlide’s notes on the format say “Seemingly the Z offset from the center focal plane (in nm?)”. In the public slides read for this, every level has it as one SLONG: 0 in those of one plane, and −100, 0 and 100 in the 3 planes of each of the 7 levels of SR1274-908A.ndpi of OME’s sample images.

3. Levels¶

Images. The images are the main-chain IFDs whose Magnification is positive, in chain order. Each MUST be one of two kinds, and every image MUST have the Compression of the first:

  • a JPEG image: Compression 7, PhotometricInterpretation 6, SamplesPerPixel 3, BitsPerSample 8 and one strip (StripOffsets and StripByteCounts of one value each), which MUST lie within the file and be at least 4 bytes long (a JPEG stream’s SOI and EOI). This is the brightfield slide.

  • a JPEG XR image: Compression 22610, which Hamamatsu uses for JPEG XR (ITU-T T.832 with its container) and tifffile names JPEGXR_NDPI. Its image is tiled (TileWidth and TileLength of at least 1, and TileOffsets and TileByteCounts) or in one strip, as a JPEG image’s, and every tile or the strip is one JPEG XR stream.

Other IFDs (the macro image, the slide map) are ignored.

Levels. The images of one Magnification are one level, the levels in the order of their Magnifications’ first images. A level is called JPEG or JPEG XR as its images are, and its size is its Magnification’s first image’s. That image MUST be strictly smaller in both width and length than the first image of the Magnification before. A JPEG image without McuStarts whose strip’s frame cannot be given the image’s size (§4, Chunks of a JPEG level) is left out; when it is the first image of all, the input is rejected.

Focal planes. A level’s planes are the images that can be one array along z, by ascending focal offset (§2). Scanners write a slide’s focal planes as images of one Magnification: SR1274-908A.ndpi has all the levels of one plane, then all those of the next. Each other image is left out, for the first of these reasons, in chain order:

reason

the image

frame

is a JPEG image whose strip’s frame cannot be given its size

size

has another size than the level’s

chunks

is not cut as the level’s first image that is not left out for the reasons above: with or without McuStarts, with another restart interval or MCU size, or with other tiles

no_offset

has no focal offset, and another image still in the level has one. When none has, the first is the level’s one plane

duplicate

has the focal offset of an image before it in the chain

offsets

is in a level whose planes’ offsets are not those kept (below)

The z axis. When no level has more than one plane, there is no z axis and no offset is compared: the levels are as they are, whatever their offsets. Otherwise every level of the hierarchy MUST have the same offsets, or z would not be one axis of the multiscale. A level is kept whole or left out whole, so the offsets kept are those that lose the least: for each list of offsets that some level’s planes have, add the pixels (width × length × planes) of the levels with exactly that list; the largest sum is kept, and of equal sums, that of the earliest level. The other levels are left out, each plane with the reason offsets.

A coarse level commonly has fewer planes than the finer ones, or just one. This keeps every plane of the finer levels and leaves such a level out. Dropping the axis instead would keep one plane of each level, and rejecting the input would keep none. What is left out stays in the source metadata (§5), and its bytes in the mirror, so nothing is lost. The multiscale stays valid: its levels have one set of axes, the same length along z, and strictly decreasing sizes. When the hierarchy’s offsets are one, it has no z axis either.

Pixels. The array has axes c, y, x, or c, z, y, x when more than one offset is kept. z comes after c, as OME-Zarr orders its axes.

  • A JPEG level has data type uint8, 3 samples, and codecs transpose and imagecodecs_jpeg (conventions §3).

  • A JPEG XR level’s data type and samples are its streams’, which the stream header’s pixel format gives (the GUID of its PixelFormat entry, §7): Gray8 (…08) is uint8 with 1 sample, Gray16 (…0B) uint16 with 1, 24bppBGR (…0C) and 24bppRGB (…0D) uint8 with 3, and 48bppRGB (…15) uint16 with 3. Any other pixel format is rejected. Its codecs are transpose (with 3 samples only) and imagecodecs_jpegxr (as the CZI convention §3.1 defines it), which decodes the samples in R, G, B order.

The tags are not the pixels. A JPEG XR level’s PhotometricInterpretation, SamplesPerPixel and BitsPerSample are not read for the layout. Hamamatsu writes 6, 3 and 8, 8, 8 whatever the streams hold: in a fluorescence slide, every level says 8-bit YCbCr with 3 samples, and every stream is 16-bit grayscale. Where the stream header and these tags disagree, the stream header decides, because a decoder gives what the stream holds. The tags are kept, as they are, in the source model (§5), and their disagreement never rejects the input. (tifffile and Bio-Formats take a CaptureMode, tag 65441, above 6 to mean 16-bit grayscale. The stream header says that too, and it also says what every other pixel format is.)

The pixel format is read from one stream of each JPEG XR image, its first stream: the strip, or the first tile whose byte count is above 0. Every image MUST have one, every image’s MUST have the pixel format of the first image’s, and its width and height MUST be its chunk’s (§4). Otherwise the input is rejected. The image’s other streams are not read, so a stream that differs from the first is not found here: what its chunk decodes to is then the decoder’s.

Scale. W0 and H0 are the first image’s, whether or not it is left out, and these tags are its too. With XResolution rx and a ResolutionUnit tag of 3 (centimeter), let p = 10000 / rx; of 2 (inch), p = 25400 / rx. If p is less than 25.4, x has unit micrometer and the first image’s scale is p; otherwise, and without a ResolutionUnit tag, no unit and scale 1, as in the TIFF convention §4.4. y likewise from YResolution. Level L’s scale is the first image’s times (W0 / WL) (x) or (H0 / HL) (y), as in the TIFF convention §4.4.

z, the same at every level, has unit micrometer and scale d / 1000 when the offsets are evenly spaced, d nanometers apart. (Exactly: the integers’ differences are all d.) Otherwise it has no unit and scale 1, since no scale can say where the planes are. The offsets are in the source metadata either way (§5).

Position. When x and y have units and the first image’s IFD has XOffsetFromSlideCenter (65422) and YOffsetFromSlideCenter (65423), in nanometres, the image’s centre is at (X / 1000, Y / 1000) micrometres, which gives its translation (conventions §5). When z has a unit, its translation is the first offset / 1000, the plane at z index 0, and otherwise 0; a translation for z alone has 0 for y and x.

omero. M has "omero": {"channels": [...]} next to multiscales, one object per sample, as in the TIFF convention §4.4, whose member order this follows. Each is "active": true with the window of its data type’s whole range ({"min": 0, "max": 255, "start": 0, "end": 255} for uint8, 65535 for uint16): refkv does not decode JPEG or JPEG XR to sample one.

  • With 3 samples (JPEG streams, and JPEG XR streams of an RGB pixel format, which decode to RGB), the samples are labeled R, G and B, with colors FF0000, 00FF00 and 0000FF.

  • With 1 sample, the channel is labeled with the first image’s Fluorescence (§2) when it is UTF-8, not empty and at most 256 bytes (up to its first NUL), and C0 otherwise, with color FFFFFF.

Chunks of planes. Each plane of a level has the chunks of its image as below, at <level>/c/0/<k>/<row>/<column>, for plane k along z, in the same chunk grid. Each chunk holds one plane.

Chunks of a JPEG XR level. Each tile is a chunk, of chunk shape [s, TileLength, TileWidth] (s the samples), and the strip of a level in one strip is the one chunk, the whole image (chunk shape [s, H, W]). The chunk holds the stream as it is. Tile j, in row-major order with ceil(W / TileWidth) tiles a row, is chunk <level>/c/0/<row>/<column>. TileOffsets and TileByteCounts MUST each have ceil(H / TileLength) × ceil(W / TileWidth) values, and a tile whose byte count is 0 has no chunk (its pixels are the fill value 0). Every other tile MUST lie within the file. As in TIFF, the tiles at the bottom and right edges are whole tiles, whose pixels outside the array are not read.

Chunks of a JPEG level. A level’s strip is a JPEG stream. Without McuStarts, the level is one chunk, the whole image (chunk shape [3, H, W]), which holds the strip. Its frame header (its first SOF0 segment) should give the size W × H, but some scanners write a frame a row short of the level’s ImageLength: a decoder then gives an image of the frame’s size, which is not the chunk’s. So, when the frame’s height h or width w differs from H or W, with an MCU of mw × mh pixels (8 times the frame’s largest horizontal and vertical sampling factors):

  • if ceil(H / mh) = ceil(h / mh), ceil(W / mw) = ceil(w / mw), and H and W are at most 65535, the chunk holds the strip with the frame’s height and width set to H and W. The stream encodes whole MCUs, so it holds every pixel of that size: rows or columns the frame left out are those the encoder padded its last MCU row or column with, and those it adds are decoded and dropped;

  • otherwise the level is not a level (§3).

A strip that has no frame header to read this way (the profile’s §7) is held as it is. With McuStarts, the strip is a sequence of restart intervals, each R MCUs of mw × mh pixels wide and one MCU tall, in row-major order, q per row and r rows (the profile defines them from the strip’s JPEG header); a chunk is a × b intervals, a = min(q, max(1, floor(1024 / (R × mw)))) across and b down, so the chunk shape is [3, b × mh, a × R × mw]. (b is chosen by the profile, as the largest that keeps every chunk’s reference, of every plane, within refkv’s limits.) Chunk (u, v) (row u, column v), at <level>/c/0/<u>/<v>, holds a complete JPEG stream of those intervals, which decodes to the chunk’s pixels; at the image’s bottom and right edges, the pixels outside the array are unspecified. Every chunk is present.

A chunk’s stream holds the strip’s header with a frame header (SOF0) of its own size, its intervals, and restart markers and an EOI of its own, so:

  • the chunk’s width a × R × mw and height b × mh MUST each be at most 65535, the largest a frame header holds, else the input is rejected;

  • the strip’s SOF0 segment, which every chunk replaces, is kept where it is: the strip is an element of the source model (§5), so the mirror rebuilds it byte for byte;

  • the strip’s own restart markers and final EOI are referenced by no chunk: each chunk puts the standard sequence that every valid JPEG stream has. The mirror keeps the strip’s own, whatever they are.

A tag that §2 treats as absent, or that has no value, is left out. With none of these, S is empty, and the root has no source metadata member (as for a brightfield slide). They are also in the source model, the values of their tags, and the images left out are there in full.

Everything else the file holds is on refkv_source, which is the file’s IR mirror (conventions §11.3). This section is the NDPI source model: the elements of an NDPI’s IR. It is the TIFF source model read in NDPI’s layout. Nothing is left out, layout and dead space included (they are elements too, the dead space as gaps): a level’s strip, its JPEG header, restart markers and EOI included, is one element, which the chunks of §4 cut along McuStarts.

5.2 Which IFDs¶

The IFDs of §2, the main chain, at depth 0 (ifds/<i>); then the IFDs that pointer tags lead to, as the TIFF source model §5.2 says, with these differences:

  • the main-chain IFDs are the only IFDs of the tree: an NDPI’s SubIFDs tag (330) is followed like the other pointer tags;

  • the other pointer tags of type IFD or IFD8 are those that are neither in the table of §2 nor layout tags, McuStarts (65426) and McuStartsHighBytes (65432) among the latter;

  • every IFD is read in NDPI’s layout (§7): one a pointer leads to is read when its offset is at least 16 and its entry count, entries, next-IFD offset and high words lie within the file, has at least one entry, and overlaps no IFD already read; an in-line LONG or IFD of count 1 leads to low + high × 2^32, and an out-of-line value is at its 64-bit offset.

5.3 Values¶

An entry’s value is an element when it lies within the file. Its bytes are in the source; the view shows it when it is a number, a record or text (ASCII) of at most 1024 bytes (conventions §8.7), and a producer reads every such value. A value element holds the bytes of its extent: an in-line LONG or IFD of count 1 shows its low 32 bits, and its high sibling the rest. Every high word is shown.

5.4 Equivalence¶

Hierarchies of one file are compared entry for entry, refkv_source included (conventions §11.4; NDPI has no named array rule).

7. NDPI profile¶

No signature. A file’s first bytes never choose this profile (spec/zarrify.md §1.2): a file the TIFF profile’s signature chose is read by it when it passes the detection test below.

Hamamatsu NDPI files are little-endian classic TIFFs (magic 42) with a 64-bit extension and pyramids stored as single JPEG strips (brightfield) or as JPEG XR tiles or strips (fluorescence, and some brightfield scanners). They are read by tiff.md §7.1 (tiff.md) with the changes below, with the tags of the convention §2 as the checked tags.

Detection. Let V be the little-endian u64 at bytes 4–11 of the header. The file is NDPI if it is little-endian with magic 42, 16 ≤ V, V + 2 ≤ the file’s size, and the IFD at V, read as below, has tag 65420 among its first n entries (its entry count n and the 12 n bytes of entries MUST lie within the file for this test; if they do not, the file is not NDPI). Otherwise it is a TIFF (tiff.md §7). This test never rejects.

IFDs. The main chain starts at V. An IFD at o is a u16 entry count n, n entries of 12 bytes as in TIFF, then a u64 next-IFD offset, then n u32 high words, one per entry. An entry’s value field is its 4 bytes plus its high word h: an out-of-line value is at offset low + h × 2^32, and an inline value of count 1 and type LONG (4) or IFD (13) is low + h × 2^32, but for the high tables OffsetHighBytes, ByteCountHighBytes (Tiles below) and McuStartsHighBytes (Strips below), whose values are the 32-bit values they store. Other inline values ignore h. An ASCII value (type 2) of count 1 or more is never in line, whatever its size: its field is the low 32 bits of its offset, and it is at low + h × 2^32, as Hamamatsu writes it (in Hamamatsu-1.ndpi of the OpenSlide test data, tag 65459’s RGB\0 is 4 bytes out of line, past 4 GiB) and as tifffile reads NDPI. Only the main chain is read for the layout: an NDPI’s SubIFDs, like the IFDs of its other pointer tags, are read in this layout for the IR only, and never reject the input (the convention §5). The limits and checks of tiff.md §7.1 apply, with these values read (tiff.md §7.1, Values read): the Magnification of every main-chain IFD, and every tag of the table of each image (the convention §3); of a scalar tag, its first value, of McuStarts, McuStartsHighBytes, BitsPerSample, TileOffsets, TileByteCounts, OffsetHighBytes and ByteCountHighBytes, every value. (A LONG or IFD value of count 1, with its high word, can exceed 2^53 − 1 too.) The strip header of each JPEG level, and the first stream’s header of each JPEG XR level, are read too, as Strips and JPEG XR streams say; so are the first image’s acquisition tags (the convention §2), when of their field types, and, when two images have one Magnification, every image’s focal offset (the convention §2). No other value is read for the layout, so the other IFDs’ tags have only their field types, counts and places checked.

Rejection. The input is rejected when a check fails, and when the convention gives it no layout: wherever the convention says that the input is rejected, or that something MUST hold and it does not.

Strips. Every image is read and checked as this section says, whether or not it is left out (the convention §3). A JPEG image’s strip is the JPEG stream S = bytes [StripOffsets, StripOffsets + StripByteCounts) of the file, which MUST lie within the file. StripByteCounts MUST be at least 4, the SOI and EOI markers of the shortest JPEG stream.

  • Without McuStarts, the level is one chunk, the whole image (chunk shape [3, H, W]). Its frame header is read from the first bytes of S: min(len(S), 1024) of them, then, while the header continues past the n bytes read and the k bytes it needs are at most c = min(len(S), 2^20), min(c, max(k, 2 × n)) of them. The frame header is the first SOF0 (C0) segment when S starts with SOI (FF D8), then, after any FF fill bytes, each marker FF xx has a 2-byte length of at least 2, every segment before the SOF0 is one of DQT, DHT, DAC, JPG, DRI, DNL, DHP, EXP, APPn, JPGn or COM (xx = C4, C8, CC, DB to DF, E0 to FD, FE), and the SOF0 segment is 10 + 3 × Nf bytes long with Nf ≥ 1 components and sampling factors whose largest horizontal and vertical are above 0. Otherwise (another marker first, such as SOS or another SOFn; a header past c bytes; a malformed segment) the strip has no frame header to read, and the chunk is the single range of S: this reading never rejects the input. With a frame header of the level’s size, the chunk is the single range of S too. With one of another size (the convention §4, Chunks of a JPEG level), when the level’s size needs the same MCU rows and columns, the chunk is three parts: the range of S up to the frame’s height field (its SOF0 segment’s bytes 5 and 6), a literal of 4 bytes, H and W as big-endian u16, and the range of S after the frame’s width field. Otherwise the image is left out (the convention §3), and when it is the first image the input is rejected.

  • With McuStarts, let M[i] be its values, plus McuStartsHighBytes[i] × 2^32 when that tag is present (it MUST then have as many values); they are offsets in S. McuStarts of one value in line, of type LONG, already has its high bits from its entry’s high word (IFDs above): its McuStartsHighBytes, when present, MUST hold that word, and is not added to it. S’s header is its first M[0] bytes: a JPEG SOI, then marker segments up to and including SOS, which MUST end exactly at M[0]. The header MUST have exactly one SOF0 (FF C0) segment and a DRI (FF DD) segment with restart interval R > 0 (in MCUs). The SOF0 segment’s sampling factors give the MCU size mw × mh: 8 times the largest horizontal and vertical factors. The strip is then a sequence of intervals, each R MCUs wide and one MCU tall, in row-major order: q = ceil(W / (R × mw)) per row and r = ceil(H / mh) rows, and McuStarts MUST have q × r values, increasing, with M[q × r − 1] < len(S). Interval i is the bytes [M[i], M[i + 1] − 2) of S ([M[i], len(S) − 2) for the last), dropping the 2-byte restart marker or EOI after it; each MUST be non-empty.

Chunks of a strip with McuStarts. A chunk is a × b intervals: a = min(q, max(1, floor(1024 / (R × mw)))) intervals across and b intervals down, so the chunk shape is [3, b × mh, a × R × mw]. a × R × mw MUST be at most 65535. The 2 bytes of S before M[i] (for i ≥ 1) and its last 2 bytes, its restart markers and EOI, are not referenced: the chunks put their own, the standard sequence FF D0 + (t mod 8) and FF D9, and the mirror keeps the strip’s (the convention §5). b is the largest number from 1 to min(r, floor(1024 / mh)) (at least 1) for which every chunk’s payload is at most 65519 bytes (§1.2); b × mh MUST be at most 65535 (it always is, since mh ≤ 120). A level’s planes have one chunk grid, so with more than one image of a Magnification, a level’s b is the largest, at most each of its planes’ own, for which every chunk of every plane is at most that, and the input is rejected when none is. Chunk (u, v) (row u, column v) is the JPEG stream NdpiMcuChunk, whose pieces its table numbers.

Clamping repeats the last row or column of intervals in the chunks at the image’s bottom and right edges; those pixels lie outside the array and are not read. (Every interval decodes on its own after a restart marker.) Chunk keys are <level>/c/0/<u>/<v>, with <k>/ before <u> when there is a z axis. The data sources are numbered by §1.2’s order of first use, over the references in the order of their keys. An image left out has no references, so adds no data source.

Data sources. Pieces 1 and 3 are the same in every chunk of a level, and are held once, in data sources, so that reading a chunk reads only its intervals from the file. They sit at the start of the strip, far from most intervals, so as ranges of the file they would cost a reader a separate request per chunk. By §1.2’s order of first use, the pieces 1 and 3 of the McuStarts level whose chunk key sorts first are sources 1 and 2; another level adds a source only for a piece whose bytes differ from every earlier one, as does another plane. (In CMU-1.ndpi every level shares piece 1, its quantization and Huffman tables, and piece 3, DRI and SOS, differs by its restart interval.) Both are JPEG markers and tables, not pixel data (§1.2, Structure only).

Tiles. A JPEG XR level’s tile j is at offset TileOffsets[j] + OffsetHighBytes[j] × 2^32 and is TileByteCounts[j] + ByteCountHighBytes[j] × 2^32 bytes long, each high table counting as 0s when absent. A high table’s values are the 32-bit values it stores: one value in line is its 4 bytes, without its entry’s high word. A high table that is present MUST have as many values as TileOffsets. A table of one value in line, of type LONG, already has its high bits from its entry’s high word (IFDs above): its high table, when present, MUST hold that word, and is not added to it. (TileOffsets, a LONG array, holds only offsets below 2^32, so Hamamatsu writes the high tables with it; in a file of less than 4 GiB they are 0s.) The sums are what the layout and the source model’s tiles use (the convention §5.1), for every IFD whose tile tables and high tables are read.

JPEG XR streams. A JPEG XR level’s first stream (the convention §3) is the bytes S of its strip, or of its first tile whose byte count is above 0. Its header is read from the first bytes of S: min(len(S), 1024) of them, then, while the header continues past the n bytes read and the k bytes it needs are at most c = min(len(S), 2^16), min(c, max(k, 2 × n)) of them. The header is the container of the CZI profile §7.4, JPEG XR: S starts with 49 49 BC 01, the u32 at byte 4 is the offset of the image directory, and of its first entries with tags BC01 (PixelFormat), BC80 (ImageWidth) and BC81 (ImageHeight) all are present, PixelFormat of type 1 and count 16 (its offset giving the 16-byte GUID), and the sizes of type 3 or 4, count 1 and at least 1. The pixel format is the GUID: the common prefix 24 C3 DD 6F 03 4E FE 4B B1 85 3D 77 76 8D C9 and the last byte of the convention §3. The input is rejected when a level has no first stream, when the header is not there to read (another start, a missing or malformed entry, or bytes past c), when its pixel format is another, or is not the first image’s, and when its ImageWidth and ImageHeight are not TileWidth and TileLength (tiled) or W and H (one strip).

Chunks of a JPEG XR level. Chunk <level>/c/0/<row>/<column> (with <k>/ before <row> when there is a z axis) is the single range of tile j = row × ceil(W / TileWidth) + column, or, for a level in one strip, <level>/c/0/0/0 the single range of S. A tile of byte count 0 has no reference.

This section is not part of the convention: nothing it specifies is written in an output.
