GRIB¶

refkv turns a file into a Zarr hierarchy by writing a small archive of references to the file’s bytes, without copying its values (overview). This page is about GRIB edition 2, the format of most operational weather forecasts.

Who this is for¶

NOAA and ECMWF publish their forecasts as GRIB2 files in public buckets: GFS, HRRR, GEFS, NAM and NBM on AWS, and ECMWF’s open data on its own server and on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure. A GRIB file is a sequence of messages, each one field (a 2-D grid of one variable, at one level and one time) packed on its own. Reading one variable means finding its message among hundreds, and cfgrib does that by downloading the file and indexing it. refkv reads only the messages’ headers (about 0.3% of a file) and writes an archive in which every Zarr chunk is one message, decoded when it is read by the refkv.grib codec. A reader then fetches only the fields it opens, from the original file.

What you get¶

A Zarr group per grid (grid0, and grid1, … when a file mixes grids), each a dataset that xarray opens (xr.open_zarr(store, group="grid0")), with:

  • Variables: one array per kind of field: the fields whose parameter, level type, product and grid agree. They are named by the parameter’s short name (ecCodes’), the level type and, for a statistic, the statistic and its period: t_isobaric, u_heightAboveGround, tp_surface_accum6h, ceil_cloudCeiling_problt152p4 (a probability), t_heightAboveGround_p90 (a percentile).

  • Dimensions: time (the reference time), step, the level (named by its type, such as isobaric in Pa or heightAboveGround in m) when the fields have one, number for an ensemble member, then the grid: latitude and longitude (regular or Gaussian), rlat and rlon (rotated), y and x (Lambert, polar stereographic or Mercator), or point (reduced and unstructured grids, one dimension of points). time and step are always there, of length 1 for a single forecast hour, so that the files of a forecast run line up.

  • Values: float64, as ecCodes decodes them (refkv’s decoder matches ecCodes value for value on every fixture and sample), NaN where a field’s bitmap or packing marks a point missing. The rows are in the file’s order: GFS and ECMWF run north to south, HRRR south to north.

  • Coordinates: time, step and valid_time (CF units, which xarray decodes), the levels, and the grid’s positions as CF scale_factor and add_offset over an index (a few kilobytes whatever the grid’s size). A Gaussian grid’s latitudes are listed, as are the latitude and longitude of a reduced grid’s points. An unstructured grid’s point has no positions: its group names the grid file that does, by its UUID.

  • Georeferencing: the GeoZarr proj:wkt2 (the CRS, on the file’s Earth shape) and spatial:transform, as for Sentinel-2, where the grid’s points are evenly spaced.

  • Source metadata: the count of messages and fields, the index the run used, and the fields that are not presented, with their reasons, under refkv_zarrified.grib. Every message’s sections are in the IR mirror on refkv_source, from which the file is rebuilt byte for byte.

Packings. Simple (5.0), complex (5.2), complex with spatial differencing (5.3, NOAA’s), IEEE floats (5.4), JPEG 2000 (5.40, which NOAA’s RAP uses), PNG (5.41) and CCSDS (5.42, ECMWF’s).

Not presented. A field is listed with its reason, rather than rejecting the file, when its grid is not one of those below, its packing is not one of the above, its product template has dimensions refkv does not name yet, or its bitmap is predefined.

Indexes. NOAA publishes <file>.idx beside each file, and ECMWF <file>.index. refkv reads one when it is there and asks for every message’s header at once, rather than walking the file a message at a time. The index is a hint: every message is read and checked either way, and the archive is the same.

Supported and not supported¶

Supported:

  • GRIB edition 2 files of one or more messages, a message holding one field or several, with NOAA’s .idx or ECMWF’s .index beside the file or without one.

  • Regular latitude/longitude (3.0), rotated (3.1), Gaussian (3.40, as GFS’s and GDAS’s fluxes and CFS use), Lambert conformal (3.30), polar stereographic (3.20, as HRRR Alaska and NBM Alaska use), Mercator (3.10, as NBM Hawaii uses) and unstructured (3.101, as ICON uses) grids.

  • Reduced grids (3.0 and 3.40 with a list of each parallel’s points, as ERA5’s native fields) that go round the Earth, as one dimension of points. A sub-area of a reduced grid is not presented.

  • Simple, complex, complex with spatial differencing, IEEE, PNG and CCSDS packing, bitmaps in the message or reused from an earlier field of the same message.

Not presented, each field listed with its reason under refkv_zarrified.grib: other grids (such as the spectral 3.50, and NCEP’s rotated E and B grids 3.32768 and 3.32769), other packings, product templates with dimensions refkv does not name yet, and predefined bitmaps.

Rejected:

  • GRIB edition 1, and files that do not parse as edition 2 messages: a truncated message, one without its 7777 end, sections out of order or over 1 MiB (spec §8.4).

  • A file none of whose fields is presented.

Example¶

example.py zarrifies an HRRR forecast from NOAA’s bucket and plots its 2 m temperature with xarray. Its dependencies are in its header (PEP 723), so it runs on its own (uv builds refkv from GitHub, which needs a Rust toolchain, until a release on PyPI has save_vzip; refkv 0.2.0 does not):

uv run docs/zarrify/grib/example.py

The profile is spec/zarrify/grib.md, and the codec spec/zarrify/grib.md §3.4.
