MP4¶

The Zarr layout of an MP4 or QuickTime file (.mp4, .m4a, .m4v, .mov: ISO/IEC 14496-12, 14496-14 and Apple’s QuickTime file format), and the translation of its boxes and tracks into JSON. What all of refkv’s conventions share is in spec/conventions.md, cited here as “conventions §n”. How refkv produces this layout as a reference store, and which files it accepts, is the MP4 profile, Part 2 below.

Convention version: 0 (until release, conventions §1) · UUID: bbe3f6b9-488d-4d8d-85ce-60a1649dd27e · Schema: schema.json

Changes: changes/mp4/, one file per change.

Added 2026-10-23.

This document has two parts (conventions §11.1).

An MP4 file is not an image. The hierarchy holds each track it zarrifies as a group: an H.264, HEVC, AV1 or VP9 video track as the Y, U and V planes of its pictures and their presentation times, a Motion JPEG track as its pictures and their presentation times, an AAC, MP3 or PCM audio track as its samples. The pictures and samples stay in the file as coded frames; the arrays’ codec, refkv.stream (conventions §10) or imagecodecs_jpeg, decodes them when a chunk is read. A PCM track’s samples are not coded, and its chunks are the file’s bytes.

2. Boxes¶

An MP4 file is a sequence of boxes. A box is a header, a 32-bit big-endian size and a 4-byte type, then its payload: size bytes in all, header included. A size of 1 is followed by a 64-bit largesize, the box’s size, so the header is 16 bytes; a size of 0 makes the box run to the end of its parent (of the file, for a top-level box). The boxes whose payload is boxes, the containers, are moov, trak, mdia, minf, stbl, edts, dinf, tref, mvex, a top-level moof and a traf in one. Any other box in a moof box than traf, and any other in a traf box than tfhd, tfdt and trun, is opaque. A full box’s payload starts with a byte of version and 3 bytes of flags. All numbers are big-endian.

The convention reads these boxes. Types are those of conventions §8.6; n is a table’s entry count, from the box’s fields.

3. Tracks and samples¶

Each trak box of the moov box is a track, numbered by its order among them from 0 (its index T); its ID is its tkhd box’s track_id. Its handler is the handler_type of its first hdlr box, the mdia box’s (a QuickTime minf box holds another, the data handler’s). Its media timescale and duration are its mdhd box’s; its edit list its elst box’s entries.

A track’s samples, numbered from 0 in decode order, are given by its sample table (the stbl box): sample j is size(j) bytes, its stsz box’s sample_size, or the table’s entry j when that is 0. The samples are in chunks: chunk c (from 1) starts at entry c − 1 of stco or co64, and holds the samples_per_chunk of the stsc entry whose first_chunk is the greatest at most c, one after the other. Sample j’s decode time is the sum of the sample_deltas of the samples before it (stts gives each run of samples its delta), and its presentation time its decode time plus its ctts offset (0 without a ctts box), both in the media timescale. The offset is signed in either version of the box, as QuickTime’s are and as Apple’s encoder writes them, negative ones in a version 0 box, where ISO/IEC 14496-12 §8.6.1.3 has version 0’s unsigned. The sync samples are the samples stss names (its entries, less 1), or every sample of the tables when the track has no stss box.

Fragments. In a fragmented file the samples go on in moof boxes, each holding traf boxes, a track fragment of the track whose ID its tfhd box names (ISO/IEC 14496-12 §8.8). A track’s samples after those of its tables are its track fragments’, in the file’s order, each’s in the order of its trun boxes and their tables. Of a sample of a track fragment:

  • Its size, duration and flags are, of the first that has one, its entry in the trun box’s table (for the flags of a run’s first sample, the run’s first_sample_flags before that); the tfhd box’s default; and the trex box’s whose track_id is the track’s, the first of them. Its composition offset is its entry’s, or 0, and signed in either version of the box, as in ctts.

  • Its offset in the file: a track fragment’s base is the tfhd box’s base_data_offset when it has one; else, when its flags have default-base-is-moof (0x020000), the start of the moof box; else the start of the moof box for the first track fragment of a moof box, and where the data of the track fragment before ended for another. A run’s data starts at the base plus its data_offset, or, without one, at the base for the first run of a track fragment and where the run before ended for another. Its samples follow each other from there. (ffmpeg’s demuxer starts a run without a data_offset at the base, whichever run it is; this document follows ISO/IEC 14496-12 §8.8.8.3.)

  • Its decode time: the first sample’s is the tfdt box’s base_media_decode_time when the track fragment has one, and else follows the sample before, as every other does: the decode time of the sample before plus its duration. Its presentation time is its decode time plus its offset.

  • It is a sync sample unless its flags have sample_is_non_sync_sample (0x00010000) set or sample_depends_on (bits 24 and 25) equal to 1, as ffmpeg’s demuxer has it: a writer may set the one and not the other.

4. The layout¶

The hierarchy is a group at the root, {"zarr_format": 3, "node_type": "group", "attributes": A}, where A has zarr_conventions and refkv_zarrified (§1) and nothing else; a group tracks, with no attributes; and, for each track the profile zarrifies (spec/zarrify.md §8.2), a group tracks/<id>, <id> the track’s ID, which declares the convention with the track’s source metadata, and its arrays below. Every array’s zarr.json is conventions §3’s, with the fill value 0.

4.1 Video tracks¶

An H.264, HEVC, AV1 or VP9 track of N samples, W × H pixels (its sample entry’s width and height), of D bits a sample (its SPS’s bit depth, or that of its av1C or vpcC box, §8.2), holds its pictures in presentation order: picture p is the sample with the p-th smallest presentation time. Its arrays are y, u and v, the planes of the pictures (y alone of a 4:0:0 HEVC track, whose pictures have no chroma), and time, the pictures’ presentation times.

Chunks. A chunk holds at most F = min(256, max(1, floor(2^25 / (W × H × B)))) pictures, B the bytes of a sample (1 for D = 8, 2 for more): at most 32 MiB of luma (16 pictures at 1920 × 1080 of 8 bits, 8 of 10), since a chunk decodes whole. The chunks are cut at the sync samples’ presentation indexes: chunk q holds the pictures a_q to a_{q+1} − 1, from a_0 = 0, where a_{q+1} is N when N − a_q ≤ F; else the greatest presentation index of a sync sample that is greater than a_q and at most a_q + F; else a_q + F. So a chunk holds whole groups of pictures and the next starts with a sync sample, but where a group longer than F is split. A sync sample’s leading pictures (those after it in decode order and presented before it) are in the chunk before it. Its key is <plane>/c/<q>/0/0.

The root’s and each track group’s source metadata are objects with the members below (Mp4Root, Mp4VideoTrack, Mp4JpegTrack, Mp4AudioTrack), in that order, translated by conventions §6. 7. What the layout does not express¶

This section is informative.

  • The movie’s timeline. The edit list maps the media timeline onto the movie’s: it delays a track, cuts its priming, or plays part of it again. The layout holds the media timeline and records the edit list, so a reader aligning two tracks applies it.

  • Color. The planes are the decoder’s Y, U and V samples. Their color primaries, transfer function, matrix and range (the SPS’s VUI, a colr box) are in the mirror; converting to RGB is the reader’s. So is an HDR stream’s transfer function: an iPhone’s HDR video is 10-bit HEVC with HLG’s, and its Dolby Vision metadata (a dvcC or dvvC box, and RPU NAL units in the samples, which an HEVC decoder ignores) is in the mirror, not applied: the planes are the HLG base layer’s.

  • Orientation. The tkhd box’s matrix, which turns a phone’s portrait video upright on the movie’s display, is in the mirror, not applied: the planes are as coded.

  • Other tracks and codecs. AV1 of the Professional and High profiles, Motion JPEG B, Dolby Vision’s own sample entries (dvh1, dvhe), 4:2:2 H.264, 4:4:4 H.264, HEVC and VP9, 24-bit PCM, which bytes has no data type for, other audio codecs, timecode, text, chapter and metadata tracks are recorded in tracks with the reason the profile does not zarrify them, and kept whole in the mirror.

  • Metadata. udta and meta boxes (titles, iTunes tags, GPS) are opaque in the mirror.

Part 2. The profile¶

The MP4 profile of spec/zarrify.md is §8 of this document. What every profile’s Part 2 shares is in spec/zarrify.md, Profiles. The profile also says which tracks are zarrified.

8. MP4 profile¶

8.1 Boxes¶

The input is an MP4 or QuickTime file, fragmented or not, which its signature (spec/zarrify.md §1.2, tested at the precedence of profiles/mp4.json, before MRC’s) chose: bytes 0–3 of H are a 32-bit big-endian size of at least 8, or 1, and bytes 4–7 are ftyp, moov, mdat, wide, free or skip. A zarrifier reads the top-level boxes’ headers, then the whole moov box and each whole moof box (and the ftyp box). It does not read the media data, though a read for a box’s header may run into it (from the first read and, once the file has shown a moof box, 4 KiB from each box whose header is not yet read, which holds most moof boxes and the mdat header after them, so that a fragment takes one read), but for a Motion JPEG track’s first sample, up to its frame header and of at most 1 MiB (§8.2): no other sample is read, so that the others’ are taken to be the same.

Checks. A zarrifier MUST reject the input unless all of these hold. They are checked as the boxes are met, the top-level boxes first, in the file’s order, then the moov box’s tree, then each track’s samples; a rejection gives the first that fails.

  • Size: the file is at least 8 bytes.

  • Boxes: every box, top-level and in a container or a sample entry, is at least its header’s length, and ends within its parent (the file, for a top-level box). Bytes after the last box of a container or a sample entry that are too few for a box header (fewer than 8) are its rest; at the top level they are a gap.

  • Brands: every top-level ftyp box’s payload, after its header of 8 or 16 bytes, holds its 8 bytes of fields (§2): a 20-byte ftyp box with a 64-bit size is rejected with the ftyp box at 0 has 4 bytes, too few for its 8 bytes of fields. The first ftyp box of at most 4096 bytes is read, for its fields, its brands and brand (§5.1); any other is opaque.

  • Fragments’ size: the file’s moof boxes together are at most 64 MiB.

  • One movie: the file has exactly one moov box, of at most 64 MiB.

  • Tables: in every stsd, stsz, elst, stts, ctts, stsc, stco, co64, stss, trex, tfhd, tfdt and trun box, the fields fit in the payload, and the table’s n entries in the bytes after them. (An mvhd, tkhd, mdhd or hdlr box too short for its fields is opaque instead.)

  • Track fragments: in the file’s order, every traf box has a tfhd box, whose track ID is a track’s of the moov box with a trex box; and every trun box’s data starts within the file (from its start to its end).

  • Samples: for every track: at most 2^24 samples, with its track fragments’, but for a PCM track of one sample size, which has an element a chunk of its table and a trun box with no table (§5.3); and for one with an stsz box: its stts box, its ctts box (when it has one) and its sample-to-chunk layout count as many samples as its stsz box; its stsc table is not empty (when it has samples), starts at chunk 1, its first_chunks increase and are at most the chunk count, and no entry has 0 samples per chunk; and every sample ends within the file.

  • A track: the profile zarrifies at least one track (§8.2). Without one, the input is rejected with each track’s reason: no track can be zarrified: track 1 (hvc1): HEVC with chroma format 3 and bit depth 8 is not supported: only 4:0:0, 4:2:0 or 4:2:2 of 8, 10 or 12 bits is.

A box that is not well formed but within a box the profile does not parse is not checked: its payload is opaque.

8.2 Tracks¶

A track is zarrified unless one of these holds, checked in this order; the first that holds is its reason (the convention §5.1), and the track is kept in the mirror but has no group:

  • its handler is neither vide nor soun (a text track);

  • its sample sizes are in an stz2 box;

  • it has no samples;

  • its stsd box has more than one sample entry, or an stsc entry or a track fragment (its tfhd box’s sample_description_index, or by default its trex box’s) names another;

  • its media timescale is 0;

  • video: its sample entry is none of avc1, hvc1, hev1, av01 and vp09; or, for avc1, its avcC box is absent, holds no SPS, or an SPS the profile cannot read, or the SPS is not 4:2:0 of 8 or 10 bits (chroma_format_idc 1 and both bit depths 8 or both 10; profiles below High are 8-bit 4:2:0); or, for av01, its av1C box is absent or cannot be read, or is not 4:2:0 of 8 or 10 bits (AV1’s Main profile); or, for vp09, its vpcC box is absent or cannot be read, or is not 4:2:0 or 4:2:2 of 8, 10 or 12 bits; or, for hvc1 and hev1, its hvcC box is absent, does not hold a VPS, an SPS and a PPS, gives NAL unit lengths of 3 bytes (lengthSizeMinusOne 2, which ISO/IEC 14496-15 does not allow), or its first SPS cannot be read to its bit depths (ITU-T H.265 §7.3.2.2), or the SPS is not 4:0:0, 4:2:0 or 4:2:2 of 8, 10 or 12 bits (chroma_format_idc 0, 1 or 2 and both bit depths 8, both 10 or both 12, or for 0 the luma bit depth alone: Main, Main 10 and range extensions); or its width or height is 0; or its stts box and track fragments do not give each of its samples a time (a description with no pts, §8.5); or its first sample is not a sync sample; or its sync samples do not increase, or one is past its last sample; or two samples share a presentation time; or two sync samples (or the last one and the end) are more than 1024 samples apart; or a sample before a sync sample in decode order is presented after it; or a sample after a sync sample in decode order is presented before the sync sample before it. So a sample after a sync sample in decode order may be presented before it (a leading picture), but after the sync sample before: a camera’s B-frames after each IDR picture are, and an open group’s, which may reference the group before (§8.3);

  • Motion JPEG: its sample entry is jpeg, mjpa, dmb1, or mp4v with an esds box of object type indication 0x6C; and its first sample has no JPEG frame header in its first 1 MiB; or its width or height is 0; or the frame header (ITU T.81 §B.2.2) is not baseline, extended sequential or progressive of 8 bits (marker 0xC0 to 0xC2); or has other than 1 or 3 components; or its size is not the sample entry’s, as an interlaced picture’s fields are, a JPEG stream each; or its stts box and track fragments do not give each of its samples a time; or two samples share a presentation time. mjpb (Motion JPEG B, whose streams have no markers) and any other mp4v are other video codecs;

  • audio: its sample entry is a QuickTime sound description of version 2, unless of type lpcm; or it is none of mp4a, .mp3 and the PCM entries below; or, for a PCM entry, the table below refuses it, or a sample is not a whole number of frames in bytes (but for an stsz box’s one sample size of 1, QuickTime’s for one frame of any size); or its sample rate (the sample entry’s, or the media timescale when that is 0, as a sample entry’s 16 bits may have to be) or channel count is 0; or, for mp4a, its esds box (or the one in its wave box) is absent or cannot be read, or its object type indication is neither AAC (0x40, 0x66 to 0x68) nor MPEG audio (0x69, 0x6B); or, for AAC, its AudioSpecificConfig cannot be read, or its object type is not AAC-LC (2), alone or under SBR’s and parametric stereo’s (HE-AAC, explicitly 5 and 29, or backward-compatibly by the sync words 0x2B7 and 0x548, ISO/IEC 14496-3 §1.6.5), or its frames are 960 samples; or its SBR’s sample rate is not twice the core’s; or its sample rate or channel count is 0;

  • its track ID is an earlier zarrified track’s (its track ID n is an earlier zarrified track's): a zarrified track’s group is named by its ID, so of the tracks that share one, only the first that no other reason keeps from being zarrified is zarrified.

HEVC’s random access pictures. ISO/IEC 14496-15 marks an HEVC sample as a sync sample when its picture is an IRAP picture: an IDR, a CRA or a BLA picture. An IRAP picture may be followed in decode order by leading pictures: RADL pictures, which are predicted from it alone, and RASL pictures, which may also reference the group before (a CRA picture’s in an open group, as x265 codes by default). The profile reads no sample, so it does not see which kind an IRAP or a leading picture is; the presentation-order rules above place them as they place H.264’s, and a chunk that holds a leading picture decodes from the sync sample before its own (§8.3), which decodes a RASL picture as a decoder of the whole stream does, and a RADL picture too (its reference pictures, from the IRAP picture on, are all decoded). A chunk that starts with a CRA picture decodes from it, and the decoder skips that picture’s RASL pictures, which are the chunk before’s. A BLA picture’s RASL pictures are output by no decoder, in a chunk or in the whole stream, so their rows are the fill value. An hev1 track may carry parameter sets in its samples as well as in its hvcC box: a chunk decodes with the box’s and with those of the samples it decodes, so a sync sample that needs a parameter set only an earlier sample carries is decoded wrongly, which the profile, reading no sample, does not check.

The presentation-order rules bound the samples a chunk decodes (§8.3). Its pictures are decoded at or after the last sync sample presented at or before its first picture, since a sample decoded before a sync sample is presented before it. And it decodes from the last sync sample at or before its first picture in decode order or from the sync sample before that, never further back, since the earlier sync sample’s leading pictures are decoded before the later one and so are not in the chunk. With the spacing rule, a chunk decodes fewer than 2048 samples before its first picture in decode order, so its reference fits in a payload (§1.2).

8.3 Chunks¶

Every chunk of every array of a zarrified track is present. A chunk of a plane or of samples is the refkv.stream chunk (conventions §10) of these samples, in decode order, a range per run of samples that follow each other in the file:

  • Video, chunk q of the pictures a = a_q to b − 1, b = a_{q+1} (the convention §4.1): the samples from a sync sample s to the last of those pictures in decode order, where s is the last sync sample at or before the first of them in decode order, or, while one of them is a leading picture of s (§8.2), the sync sample before s. A sample’s position is its presentation index less a when that is from 0 to b − a − 1, else −1. So the chunk decodes the group of pictures it starts in from its sync sample, and every picture its own pictures are predicted from, an open group’s leading pictures included; the pictures it decodes and does not hold (before a, or after b − 1 but decoded before one of its own) are dropped. A chunk that starts with a sync sample decodes from it. The chunks of u and v are y’s.

  • Audio, chunk q of the frames a = q × 256 to b − 1: the frames l to b − 1, frame j’s position −1 when j < a and (j − a) × S otherwise. l = a for the first chunk; otherwise, for AAC, l = a − 1 (the frame before the chunk, whose overlap its first frame needs), for HE-AAC l = max(0, a − 64) (SBR and parametric stereo decode only after their headers, which AudioToolbox’s streams were measured to need up to 32 frames to reach, 64 frames being about 3 seconds at a 22.05 kHz core; the profile, reading no sample, does not see where one is, and a chunk whose lead-in holds none lacks them up to the next), and for MP3, l is the greatest index at most a − 2 such that frames l to a − 3 hold at least 511 bytes of main data, counting each frame’s size less 38 bytes (its header, CRC and side information are at most that), or 0 when none does: the MP3 profile’s rule (zarrify/mp3.md §8.2) with MPEG-1’s reservoir and a bound in place of each frame’s side information, which a zarrifier does not read.

8.5 The description¶

This section is not part of the convention: nothing it specifies is written in an output. The parser returns, beside the IR, a description of the file: what it found of the source, for a projection to read with the IR and its stored arrays (spec/stored-array.md §7). It holds none of an output’s decisions: which tracks are zarrified and each one’s reason (§8.2), and the arrays’ data types, shapes, chunks and codecs (§4) are the projection’s. Its JSON Schema is mp4/description.json. It is a JSON object with these members:

member

value

brand

the ftyp box’s brands, an Mp4Brand, or null without one

timescale, duration

the mvhd box’s

tracks

each track, in the order of the trak boxes, as below

A track has these members:

member

value

index

its position among the trak boxes

id

its tkhd box’s track ID

handler

its hdlr box’s handler type, as text

timescale, duration

its mdhd box’s

edits

its elst box’s entries, each an Mp4Edit

samples

its samples: its tables’, then its track fragments’ (§3)

sizes

its samples’ distinct sizes, in bytes, in the order each first appears

bytes

its samples’ bytes

stz2

whether its sample sizes are in an stz2 box

stsz_sample_size

its stsz box’s sample_size, or null when the box has none or gives each sample’s

sample_descriptions

its stsd box’s entry count

entries

the sample entries the profile read

other_description

whether an stsc entry or a track fragment names a sample description other than the first

entry

its first sample entry, as below, or null without one

pts

of a vide track, or one whose sample entry is a Motion JPEG one (§8.2), each sample’s presentation time in its timescale, in decode order (its stts and ctts boxes’, then its fragments’), when its stts box and fragments count its samples; else null

sync

of a track that has pts, its sync samples’ indexes in decode order, from 0 (every sample when it has no stss box); else null

elements

the ids of IR elements (§5.3) by name: trak, its trak box, which keys a vide or soun track’s stored arrays (spec/stored-array.md §7), and first, its first sample’s data element, which the others follow, or null without samples

and a sample entry these:

member

value

type

its type, as text: avc1, mp4a, sowt and so on

width, height

a visual sample entry’s, else 0

channels, sample_rate, sample_size, sound_version

an audio sample entry’s channel_count, sample rate (a version 2 QuickTime sound description’s audio_sample_rate), sample_size (its const_bits_per_channel) and version, else 0

lpcm_flags, lpcm_packet

a version 2 QuickTime sound description’s format_specific_flags and const_bytes_per_audio_packet, else 0

video_config

the configuration box its type names, avcC of avc1, hvcC of hvc1 and hev1, av1C of av01 and vpcC of vp09: box, its type; data, its payload in base64; and fields, what the profile reads of it (profile, level, chroma, the chroma_format_idc, and luma_bits and chroma_bits, the bit depths), or null when it cannot; null for another type, or without the box

esds

its esds box’s decoder configuration (in a QuickTime wave box or not): object_type_indication, config, the decoder specific information in base64, and aac, the AudioSpecificConfig’s object_type, sample_rate, channels, short_frames (960-sample frames), sbr_sample_rate (null without SBR) and parametric_stereo, or null when it cannot be read; object_type_indication, config and aac each null when the box cannot be read; null without one

pcmC

its pcmC box’s format_flags and sample_size, or null

enda

its enda box’s flag, true for little-endian, or null

jpeg

of a Motion JPEG sample entry, its track’s first sample’s JPEG frame header (ITU T.81 §B.2.2): marker, precision, width, height and components, or null

The projection reads the root’s and each track’s source metadata (§5) from the description, and makes refkv’s plan of the file from the description alone: tracks, each track’s reason (§8.2), null for a track it zarrifies, and of such a track its format, its samples’ geometry (the arrays’ data type among it) and what its arrays read (setup, profile, level and bit_depth of a video track, sample_rate, channels and spf of an audio one, endian, bytes and frames of a PCM one); and chunk_frames, the most frames of a chunk (§8.3). It rejects the file, with the check of §8.2, when no track is zarrified. The plan belongs to refkv’s convention, not to the IR.
