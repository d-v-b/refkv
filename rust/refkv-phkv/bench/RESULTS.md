# phkv benchmark results

Method, files and caveats: [README.md](README.md). Tables are generated by `report.py` from `results.json`.
A request is one read of the archive (a `Range` request over HTTP, a `pread` on a file).
"http 20 ms" is loopback with 20 ms added to each request.

## What they show

For lookups of a key in a cold store (open plus one lookup), over HTTP with 20 ms per request, `refs` workload:

| keys | vzip | vzip, paged | phkv | hybrid, as vzip | hybrid, as phkv |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 3 req, 68 KiB | 4 req, 68 KiB | 1 req, 33 KiB | 4 req, 68 KiB | 8 req, 65 KiB |
| 10,000 | 3 req, 691 KiB | 4 req, 70 KiB | 2 req, 65 KiB | 4 req, 70 KiB | 11 req, 68 KiB |
| 100,000 | 3 req, 6.8 MiB | 4 req, 81 KiB | 3 req, 65 KiB | 4 req, 81 KiB | 14 req, 94 KiB |
| 1,000,000 | 3 req, 69 MiB | 4 req, 200 KiB | 5 req, 345 KiB | 4 req, 200 KiB | 18 req, 345 KiB |

1. **The unpaged vzip archive's open cost grows with the key count**: it reads the whole central
   directory (69 MiB, 3.9 s at 10^6 keys, in Python). Paged vzip and phkv do not: at 10^6 keys they
   read 200 KiB and 345 KiB, in 4 and 5 requests.
2. **For a cold store, phkv and paged vzip are close.** phkv's open reads its PTRHASH and PAGES
   sections whole: they fit in the first read below about 10^4 keys, and are about 280 KiB beyond
   it at 10^6, so phkv takes 1 to 5 requests. Paged vzip takes 4 at every size. phkv takes fewer requests up
   to 10^5 keys and one more at 10^6. In time, phkv is ahead at every size (22 to 107 ms against 88 to 184 ms
   at 20 ms per request), but the vzip times include the Python reader's own work, which is 92 ms at 10^6 keys
   even on a local file, so most of that gap is not the format's.
3. **A lookup in an opened phkv store is two requests, not one.** The slot table is read at the
   key's slot, which gives the record's offset, and then the record: 2 requests and about 520 B for
   `refs`, at every size. `bytes` is 3: the record reader's first read is 512 B (`Limits::record_read`), so a record that
   holds a 1 KiB inline value needs a second read for the rest. Paged vzip fetches a page and the entry's body: about 2
   requests, fewer when the page is cached (1.6 at 10^4 keys, 2.0 at 10^5 and 10^6); unpaged vzip
   needs 1 once its whole central directory is in memory. So the "one hop" is one dependent read of a
   slot, then one of a record; it is not one request. At 20 ms that is 42 ms per lookup for phkv, 44 ms for paged
   vzip, and 22 ms for unpaged vzip.
4. **A hybrid archive read as phkv is expensive to open: 8 to 18 requests**, where the same bytes read
   as vzip take 4. The container sits before the central directory, so the superblock is at a
   distance from the end equal to the central directory's size, and the reader finds it by probing 0,
   1 KiB, 2 KiB, 4 KiB, ... one request each (phkv.md §17.2). For 10^6 keys the central directory is
   about 118 MiB, 17 probes. Each probe outside the first 64 KiB of the file's end costs a round
   trip: 378 ms at 20 ms per request, against 178 ms as vzip. A hybrid reader could
   start from the end of central directory record, as vzip readers do, and not probe; that is a
   change to §17.2 for hybrid archives, not measured here.
   Lookups after that are as in phkv, 2 requests.
5. **`list` with phkv reads one page per request.** Pages are 4 KiB. For `refs`, 100 keys fit in 1 to 2
   pages (1 to 2 requests, 4 to 8 KiB). For `bytes` with 1 KiB values inline, the same 100 keys span 26 pages: 26
   requests and 545 ms at 20 ms, against 4 requests and 75 ms for paged vzip. The pages are adjacent,
   so one request would do. (A hybrid archive does not have this: its values are in the ZIP entries, so
   its records are small: 2 requests, 42 ms.)
6. **Size.** For `refs`, per key: standalone phkv 34 B at 10^3 and 38 B at 10^6; vzip 119 to 125 B; hybrid
   172 to 225 B (the central directory plus the container). For `bytes` the 1 KiB values dominate:
   phkv is 6% smaller than vzip, a hybrid archive 6% larger.
