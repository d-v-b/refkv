MP3¶

The Zarr layout of an MP3 file (MPEG-1, MPEG-2 or MPEG-2.5 Layer III audio, .mp3), and the translation of its stream parameters and tags into JSON. What all of refkv’s conventions share is in spec/conventions.md, cited here as “conventions §n”. How refkv produces this layout as a reference store, and which files it accepts, is the MP3 profile, Part 2 below.

Convention version: 0 (until release, conventions §1) · UUID: b6cb0047-9448-4fdc-b03e-10dd3d6aa829 · Schema: schema.json

Changes: changes/mp3/, one file per change.

Added 2026-10-21.

This document has two parts (conventions §11.1).

An MP3 file is not an image: the hierarchy holds the decoded audio as one array of samples, with no OME-NGFF metadata. The samples stay in the file as MPEG frames; the array’s codec decodes them when a chunk is read.

A Layer III frame is its header, the 2-byte CRC when the protection bit is 0, the side information (MPEG-1: 17 bytes for mono, 32 otherwise; MPEG-2 and 2.5: 9 for mono, 17 otherwise), and the main data that fills the rest of the frame, M = L − 4 − 2 × crc − side bytes. A frame’s own audio may begin before its main data, in the main data of earlier frames (the bit reservoir): the side information’s main_data_begin says how many bytes back, at most 511 for MPEG-1 and 255 for MPEG-2 and 2.5. So a frame cannot be decoded alone; §4.1 says what a chunk holds so that it can be.

Two frames are of one stream when they agree on the version, the layer, the sample rate and the channel count. The bitrate, the padding, the protection and the channel mode may differ from frame to frame (a variable bitrate stream; joint stereo).

The info frame. An encoder may start the stream with a frame that holds no audio but a tag describing the stream: a Xing or Info tag (the ASCII Xing or Info right after the side information, that is at offset 4 + 2 × crc + side of the frame), followed by a 32-bit big-endian flags word and, for each flag bit set, in this order:

4. The layout¶

The hierarchy is a group at the root with one array at path samples: the root zarr.json is {"zarr_format": 3, "node_type": "group", "attributes": A}, where A has zarr_conventions and refkv_zarrified (§1) and nothing else.

4.1 The array¶

The audio frames are the stream’s frames in file order, numbered from 0, without the info frame (§2); N is their count (§8.1 says which frames those are). Every audio frame has the stream’s S and C. With F = 256 frames per chunk, chunk q holds the audio frames q × F to min(N, (q + 1) × F) − 1, which are its frames; the last chunk may hold fewer than F, and is padded to the chunk shape by the codec. The chunk grid is regular; the chunk keys are samples/c/<q>/0.

A chunk is a StreamChunk, a refkv.stream chunk of MP3 frames: its own frames, each placed at its S samples from the chunk’s start, after its lead-in, the frames before its first frame that a decoder needs to have decoded so that it decodes the chunk’s frames as a decoder of the whole stream does (§8.2 says which). The lead-in’s frames supply the bit reservoir and the overlap state of the filter bank for the chunk’s frames, and their own output is dropped.

4.2 The samples¶

Sample i of channel c is the output of a Layer III decoder, as ISO/IEC 11172-3 and 13818-3 define it, decoding the audio frames one after the other from the first (the frames alone: not the bytes between them), as a binary32 number, the ith sample it produces for channel c, from −1 to 1 (a decoder’s output before any conversion to integers); the decoder starts with an empty bit reservoir and a zero filter-bank state, and its first frame’s output is what it is (the stream’s encoder delay and the decoder’s own delay, about 529 samples, are in the array; neither is trimmed). The samples of a frame are its S outputs; a frame the decoder cannot decode (its reservoir reaches before the first audio frame) gives zeros. Two conforming decoders may differ in the last bits of a sample (the standards allow it); the convention does not say which decoder a reader uses, so long as it decodes refkv.stream (conventions §10), and spec/zarrify.md §1.1 compares references, not samples.

The sample rate is sample_rate of §5.1; sample i is at i / sample_rate seconds from the stream’s start.

The one chunk, samples/c/0/0, is an 88-byte literal, the header (16 90 01 02 90 01 80 09 ...: the count 22, then each frame’s size 144 and its position’s step, 1 for the first and 576 for each after it), then the range (0, 3168) of source 0: every frame, no lead-in. Read with 4 frames a chunk instead (as the tests do), chunk 3 holds frames 12 to 15, and its range is (1152, 1152): it starts at frame 8, since the lead-in is frames 10 and 11, the two before the chunk, and before them frames 8 and 9, whose main data (131 bytes each: 144 − 4 − 9) holds 262 bytes, at least the 255-byte reservoir of MPEG-2. Its header places frames 8 to 11 at −1 and frames 12 to 15 at 0, 576, 1152 and 1728.

7. What the layout does not express¶

This section is informative.

  • Gapless playback. The encoder delay and padding of a LAME tag say which samples at the start and the end are the encoder’s, not the recording’s; players trim them. The array holds every decoded sample and records the two counts in info, so a reader that wants the recording alone drops delay + 529 samples at the start (529 being a decoder’s own delay) and padding − 529 at the end, as players do.

  • The tags’ items. An ID3v2 tag’s frames (title, artist, cover art, lyrics) and an APE tag’s items are kept whole in the mirror and not decoded; id3v1 is, as it is fixed-width.

  • Per-frame values. The bitrate, padding and channel mode of each frame, and the seek table of a Xing tag, are in the frames and the info frame in the mirror, not in the root.

  • The decoder. Which decoder, and whether it uses the gapless information or the seek table, is the reader’s choice; the convention says what the samples are, to the accuracy the standards allow.

  • A broken reservoir. A frame whose bit reservoir reaches into frames the stream does not hold (two streams cut and joined, a frame lost) decodes as the reader’s decoder has it: some decoders skip it, which shifts the samples of its chunk by a frame; others give noise or silence for it. Only the stream’s first frames, which have no earlier frames, are such frames in a well-formed file.

Part 2. The profile¶

The MP3 profile of spec/zarrify.md is §8 of this document. What every profile’s Part 2 shares is in spec/zarrify.md, Profiles.

8. MP3 profile¶

8.1 The stream¶

The input is an MP3 file, which its signature (spec/zarrify.md §1.2, tested at the precedence of profiles/mp3.json) chose: bytes 0–2 of H are 49 44 33 (ID3), or bytes 0–3 are an MPEG audio frame header: bits 31–21 all ones, bits 20–19 not 01, bits 18–17 not 00, bits 11–10 not 11 (the convention §2; any layer and bitrate index). The whole file is read: an MP3 file has no index, and each frame’s header gives the next.

Checks. A zarrifier MUST reject the input unless all of these hold, checked in this order:

  • Size: the file is at least 4 bytes.

  • ID3v2 tags: for each tag at the start (ID3 at offset 0, and again at the end of each tag): the file has its 10-byte header; its four size bytes are each below 80; and the tag, footer included, ends within the file.

  • The stream: the file has a first confirmed frame (below), and it is Layer III. A Layer I or Layer II first frame rejects by name (MPEG-2 Layer II (the frame at 0) is not supported: only Layer III is).

  • One stream: no confirmed frame changes the version, the layer, the sample rate or the channel count of the stream (the first confirmed frame’s): the frame at 2016 changes the sample rate from 16000 to 22050.

  • Audio: at least one audio frame. Without one, the input is rejected as free-format bitrate (the frame header at <offset>) is not supported when the scan met a free-format header (the first one), else as no MPEG audio frames.

Tags. The ID3v2 tags are those at the start, one after the other, each as the convention §3 measures it; A is the offset after the last (0 without one). The ID3v1 tag is the last 128 bytes when they start with TAG and lie at or after A; E is its offset, else the file’s size. An APE footer ending at E, whose tag (the convention §3) is at least 32 bytes and starts at or after A, makes the tag end at E and moves E to the tag’s start. The frames are scanned in [A, E).

Frames. The scan keeps a position p, from A, and the stream (the first confirmed frame’s version, layer, sample rate and channel count; none at first). At p, when p + 4 ≤ E and the header at p is valid (the convention §2), with its frame length L, the frame fits when p + L ≤ E; it is consistent when there is no stream yet or it is of the stream; the next header is valid and of the same stream as this header when p + L + 4 ≤ E and the 4 bytes at p + L are a valid header that agrees with this one on the version, layer, sample rate and channel count. The frame is confirmed when it fits and one of these holds:

  • it is chained (the previous confirmed frame ended at p) and consistent;

  • there is no stream yet and p + L = E (a file of one frame);

  • the next header is valid and of the same stream as this header.

A confirmed frame that is not consistent rejects the input (the one-stream check); the first confirmed frame sets the stream (the Layer III check). A confirmed frame is a frame: p moves to p + L, and the next frame is chained. Otherwise, or when the header at p is not valid, p moves to p + 1 and the next frame is not chained. The scan ends when p + 4 > E. A valid header that is not confirmed, a frame that does not fit (the last frame, truncated), and every byte between confirmed frames are not frames.

The info frame. The first confirmed frame is the info frame when it holds a Xing, Info or VBRI tag (the convention §2); it is not an audio frame. Every other confirmed frame is an audio frame, numbered from 0 in order.

So the scan accepts a frame reached by the chain without looking ahead, as decoders do; a frame found after junk (or the first) needs the next frame to agree with it, so that a sync in junk is not a frame; a parameter change is only taken for one when two frames of the new parameters follow each other; and trailing junk, a truncated last frame and a tag the convention does not know are bytes between or after frames, kept as gaps (the convention §5.3).

8.2 Chunks¶

The array has ceil(N / F) chunks, F = 256, chunk q holding the audio frames a = q × F to b − 1, b = min(N, a + F) (the convention §4.1). Every chunk is present. Chunk q, at samples/c/<q>/0, is the refkv.stream chunk, an Mp3Chunk (conventions §10) of the audio frames l to b − 1, as a literal range for its header and ranges of source 0 for the frames: frame j’s position is −1 when j < a, and (j − a) × S otherwise. l is the first frame of the lead-in: l = a for the first chunk (a = 0); otherwise l is the greatest index at most a − 2 such that the main data of frames l to a − 3 (the convention §2, M of each) sums to at least R, where R = 511 for MPEG-1 and 255 for MPEG-2 and 2.5, or l = 0 when no index does.

Mp3Chunk, chunk q, of the audio frames l to b − 1:

part

range

presence

value

the header

literal

required

the StreamChunk header: the frames’ count b − l, then, for each frame j from l, its length and its position, −1 when j < a and (j − a) × S otherwise

the frames

source ranges (0, offset, length)

required

frames l to b − 1, in order, each its offset and length L in the file; a frame that starts where the one before it ends extends that one’s range (conventions §10)

So the lead-in is the two frames before the chunk, and before them the fewest frames whose main data holds the largest bit reservoir the version allows: a decoder that decodes the lead-in holds the reservoir the two frames need, the two frames give it the filter bank’s overlap and the synthesis state the chunk’s first frame needs (the frame before it in MPEG-1, where a frame has two granules; the two frames before it in MPEG-2 and 2.5, where a frame has one), and the chunk’s frames then decode as they do in the stream. Decoding the lead-in costs a few frames’ work per chunk and no extra reads. The bytes between frames are in no range (conventions §10 says why).

The ranges lie within the file (frames are found within [A, E)). The output has no data sources: its source table is source 0 alone. refkv_source is the IR mirror (the convention §5.2). The archive has no other entries than the root’s and the array’s zarr.json, the array’s chunks, and the mirror’s.

8.4 The description¶

This section is not part of the convention: nothing it specifies is written in an output. The parser returns, beside the IR, a description of the file: what it found of the source, for a projection to read with the IR and its stored arrays (spec/stored-array.md §7). It holds none of an output’s decisions: the frames of a chunk and the lead-ins (§8.2), and the array’s data type, shape, chunks and codecs (§4.1) are the projection’s. Its JSON Schema is mp3/description.json. It is a JSON object with these members:

The projection reads them so, and makes refkv’s plan of the file from the description alone: chunk_frames, F, and leads, the first frame l of each chunk’s lead-in, from main_data and version (§8.2). The plan belongs to refkv’s convention, not to the IR; the array’s chunks are read from it.
