GRIB: stored arrays¶

What the GRIB parser describes as stored arrays (stored-array.md), part of the GRIB profile.

Every message of a GRIB edition 2 file has descriptions, whether or not the profile presents its fields, keyed by its element (messages/<i>), a kind of element that may declare several arrays: each bitmap one of its sections 6 holds (indicator 0) and each of its fields (a section 7, with the sections 3, 5 and 6 in effect at it) is an array, indexed in the order of their sections, a bitmap before its field. The file’s store parser describes them itself (stored-array.md §6). A field’s array is the values its section 7 codes, in the file’s order: how fields stack into a variable, where on its grid a bitmap puts a field’s values, and which way a grid’s rows run are the profile’s. The facts give the keys: each entry of fields has declared_by, the id of its message’s element, array, the index of its values’ array, and mask, the index of the bitmap that says which points its values are (its own, or, under indicator 254, the message’s last one before it), or null. Below, an octet is numbered from 1 in its section, a section is the element messages/<i>/sections/<j>, and n is section 5’s number of values (octets 6–9).

  • Declarations. A field’s: its section 3 (the grid’s number of points, its template, Ni, Nj and the scanning mode); its section 5 (n, the data representation template and its octets: the reference value R at octets 12–15, the binary scale factor E at 16–17 and the decimal scale factor D at 18–19, the type of the original values, complex packing’s missing value management at octet 23 and its substitutes for missing values at 24–31); the first 6 octets of its section 6, which end with the bitmap indicator (the section’s element, or the header element of a section that holds more); and, where a bitmap says which points its values are, that bitmap’s bits (the bitmap element of its section 6). A bitmap’s: the section 3 in effect, and the first 6 octets of its section 6, likewise.

  • A bitmap is a bit for each point of the grid, in the file’s order, 1 where the point has a value: shape is section 3’s number of points (octets 7–10), one axis, and item_bits is 1. It is one unit in an empty chain, Each of the points (an empty List for none) under short, and its row is the section’s bitmap element, the octets after the first 6, or the range of its first octets that hold the points’ bits where it holds more. A bitmap whose section holds fewer bits than the grid has points has no grid.

  • A field’s shape. Where every point has a value (indicator 255, and n the grid’s number of points) and the grid is one whose rows the parser reads (template 3.0, 3.1, 3.10, 3.20, 3.30 or 3.40, defined by its template, with no list of the points of each row, and Ni × Nj its number of points), shape is Nj, then Ni, with axes [1, 0], since section 3 gives Ni first; or, where the scanning mode’s bit 3 (0x20) says that consecutive points run along a column, Ni, then Nj, with no axes. Rows that alternate in direction (bit 4, 0x10) are as the file stores them. Every other field is one axis of its n coded values, in order: one on a reduced grid, on a 3.101 grid or on a grid the parser does not read; one whose n is not the grid’s number of points; and one with a bitmap, of any indicator. A field with a bitmap is the values of the points the bitmap keeps, and no value is made for a point it leaves out: the bitmap’s own array, or for a predefined bitmap (indicator 1–253) the indicator, says which they are.

  • Units. A field is one unit: each axis Each of its length, or an empty List for an axis of length 0, under short. Its row is its section 7’s element with the range of the octets that hold its values, which start after the section’s first 5; a field of no values, and one whose values no octet codes, has no row.

  • IEEE floats (template 5.4, of the precision 1 or 2 at octet 12) are stored as they are: item_bits is 32 or 64, the elements big-endian, the chain empty, and the row’s range the n values’ octets.

  • A constant field codes no value: simple packing, JPEG 2000, PNG or CCSDS packing (5.0, 5.40, 5.41, 5.42) of a bit width of 0 (octet 20), and complex packing (5.2, 5.3) of no groups (octets 32–35). Every value is the reference value R, an IEEE binary32, big-endian: item_bits is 32, the chain is empty, and the grid is sparse, with no row, its fill given: the place of R (section 5’s octets 12–15). ecCodes and NCEP’s g2c read R itself whatever the decimal scale factor D is, as their encoders write it, where FM 92’s regulation 92.9.4 would give R × 10^−D.

  • A field in simple packing (5.0) is its integers as section 7 stores them: item_bits is the bit width (octet 20), whatever it is, the chain empty, and the row’s range the octets that hold n integers.

  • A packed field is the integers its packing codes, in the codec of the integers, each parameter a range of section 5 (stored-array.md §3.2): grib_complex for 5.2 and for 5.3, with differencing (missing value management 0; widths of at most 64 bits; an order of 1 or 2, and descriptors of at most 8 octets), item_bits 64; jpeg2k for 5.40, whose codestream’s samples, in the image’s order whatever its rows, are the unit’s integers, item_bits the bit width in the whole octets that hold it (8 × ⌈bits / 8⌉); grib_png for 5.41 and grib_ccsds for 5.42 (unsigned samples of at most 32 bits), item_bits the bit width. The row’s range is all of section 7 after its first 5 octets. The parser reads no octet of section 7 after them, so a stream that is not its codec’s, or holds other than n integers, or a PNG image of other pixels than the bit width’s, is not detected.

  • Values. The description stops at the integers X, which the profile makes the field’s values of with R, E and D, its declarations: FM 92’s regulation 92.9.4 gives (X × 2^E + R) × 10^−D. ecCodes computes it in binary64, X and R converted first, each operation rounded, in that order, with 2^E 1 multiplied by 2 as many times as E’s magnitude (divided, for a negative E), and 10^−D 1 divided by 10 as many times as D’s magnitude (multiplied, for a negative D); R is an IEEE binary32, big-endian, and E and D are each an integer whose first bit is its sign and whose other 15, the most significant first, are its magnitude.

  • source, with format grib, id the data representation template’s number and parameters the place of section 5’s octets after it (from octet 12), for any other template (the spectral 5.50 and 5.51, 5.61, 5.200), and for one of those above with parameters its codec does not take (IEEE floats of another precision, complex packing of other options, signed CCSDS samples, a section 5 shorter than its template’s octets). Complex packing with groups and missing value management 1 or 2 is source too: a coded value whose bits are all 1 (in a group of width 0, a group’s reference whose bits are all 1) is a missing value, and under 2 so is one that is 1 less, for which the field codes no integer, so that an element would have to be made for it. The field then has no item_bits, and its row’s range is section 7 after its first 5 octets.

  • No grid for a field whose section 7 holds fewer octets than its IEEE floats or its simple packing’s integers take, and for one of another packing, named or source, with values to code and a section 7 of 5 octets: no unit of no bytes is named.

  • The grid is dense, but a constant field’s: GRIB has no fill value.
